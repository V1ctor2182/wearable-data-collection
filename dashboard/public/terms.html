1. Overview

The Wearable Data Pipeline ("the Application") is a research platform operated by the Systems Engineering of Autonomy (SEA) Lab at Columbia University. It collects, stores, and processes health data from consumer wearable devices and hospital electronic health records (EHR) via the SMART on FHIR standard for the purpose of academic research.

By using this Application or authorizing it to access your data, you agree to these Terms and Conditions. If you do not agree, please do not use the Application.

2. Eligibility

You must be at least 18 years of age and capable of forming a binding agreement to use this Application. Use of this Application as a research participant is governed by the applicable Institutional Review Board (IRB)-approved consent form, which takes precedence over these Terms in the event of any conflict.

3. Data Collection and Use

The Application may collect the following categories of data upon your authorization:

  • Wearable device data — including but not limited to heart rate, sleep, activity, SpO2, HRV, steps, and other metrics from devices such as Fitbit, Oura, WHOOP, Garmin, Apple Health, Samsung Health, and Google Fit.
  • Clinical EHR data — including laboratory results, vital signs, conditions, medications, procedures, immunizations, allergies, care plans, and other FHIR R4 resources accessed via SMART on FHIR from your healthcare provider's EHR system (e.g., Epic MyChart, Cerner).

All data is collected solely for academic research purposes. Data is stored as raw payloads in a secure database with content-based deduplication. No data is sold, shared with advertisers, or used for commercial purposes.

4. Authorization and Consent

The Application accesses your health data only after you explicitly authorize the connection through OAuth 2.0 or SMART on FHIR authorization flows provided by the respective data source (wearable device platform or healthcare provider). You may revoke access at any time through your device platform's settings or your healthcare provider's patient portal.

5. Data Storage and Security

  • All data is encrypted in transit (TLS) and at rest.
  • Access tokens are stored securely and refreshed automatically; they are never exposed to end users or third parties.
  • The Application follows the principle of minimum necessary access, requesting only the FHIR scopes and API permissions required for the research protocol.
  • Data is stored on infrastructure managed by Columbia University, subject to university IT security policies.

6. Data Retention

Data is retained for the duration of the research study or as required by the applicable IRB protocol. Upon study completion, data will be de-identified or deleted in accordance with the approved research protocol and university data retention policies.
